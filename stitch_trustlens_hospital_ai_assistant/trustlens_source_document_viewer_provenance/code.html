Subject: Emergent Hypokalemia Intravenous Correction Guidelines Section 4: Nurse Autonomy Protocols

§ 4.1: Background & Clinical Indications for Hypokalemic Intervention

Acute hypokalemia in emergency presentations predisposes patients to life-threatening cardiac dysrhythmias, neuromuscular paralysis, and secondary hypomagnesemia refractory cascade. Immediate correction is mandatory upon laboratory verification. Rapid intravenous access should be preserved, with dual peripheral or approved central venous lines evaluated based on required infusion concentration.

§ 4.2 Standard Dispensing & Nurse Standing Authority

“In emergent hypokalemia, RN tier 2 or higher may dispense and administer potassium chloride IV piggyback (≤20 mEq/hr) per standing order without prior physician co-signature for adult patients presenting with confirmed serum K⁺ < 3.2 mEq/L.”

§ 4.2.2: Pre-infusion Renal Clearance Validation Stipulations

Standing nurse administration outlined in § 4.2.1 is strictly contingent on verifiable renal clearance. A confirmed serum creatinine < 1.8 mg/dL obtained within four (4) hours of presentation must be documented in the electronic medical chart. If baseline renal clearance is absent, or if acute anuria / oliguria (< 0.5 mL/kg/h over 2 consecutive hours) is observed, immediate bedside consultation with an emergency attending physician is mandatory prior to infusion priming.

§ 4.3: Pediatric & Renal Failure Exclusions

All patients under 18 years of age (pediatric triage criteria) are expressly exempted from standing order dispensation. All pediatric electrolyte interventions mandate explicit weight-based attending physician co-signature and hospital central pharmacy double-verification. Concomitant cardiac telemetry monitoring is compulsory for all rates exceeding 10 mEq/hr.
